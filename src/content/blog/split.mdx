The session is here.

That sentence doesn't carry the weight it probably should. Three words, flat, factual — but the consequences trail behind it like a train that hasn't decelerated yet. Fixed dates, institutional deadlines, material that needs to be absorbed with enough fidelity to reproduce under exam conditions. None of this is negotiable in the way that self-imposed deadlines are negotiable. It simply arrives and expects compliance.

## Studying versus building

These are not the same cognitive activity. That distinction feels obvious written down, but its practical consequences are harder to absorb. Studying asks me to receive and retain — to move through someone else's material, hold its structure, and be ready to reconstruct it on demand. Building asks me to originate: to carry a half-formed system in my head, trace its inconsistencies forward, and push it toward something that functions. Different posture. Completely different rhythm.

The day contains only the hours it contains.

## What is still running

Right now, parallel to the exam schedule: the StangaNetLib documentation site, stalled at low priority but refusing to leave the mental queue. UniShelf, the Blazor platform meant to untangle the UniPD CS Journey from the constraints of static hosting. The portfolio, which I keep revising between study sessions because leaving it incomplete bothers me in ways I cannot fully account for.

None of these feel optional in the sense I'd like them to. They represent months of structural investment. Letting them go entirely cold while the session runs its course — six, eight weeks — would feel like stepping away from scaffolding mid-construction. The work doesn't freeze cleanly. It persists.

It isn't laziness. That word doesn't describe anything happening here.

## What actually competes with preparation

The thing splitting the attention is not distraction. Not noise, not the absence of discipline. It is other work — legitimate, self-directed, meaningful in its own right. That distinction matters, because you can train focus. You can engineer an environment that forces attention in one direction and sustains it there. But when both things pulling at you are real, when both carry actual weight and actual stakes, the question of allocation stops being a productivity problem and starts being a values problem. There is no obvious element to eliminate.

## No resolution

The exams will happen on fixed dates and produce fixed outcomes. The projects will either advance or they won't, depending on how much margin survives at the edges of each week.

What I can't manufacture is a clean principle that reconciles the two halves. Some weeks the balance will settle somewhere workable. Other weeks it won't. That's the actual texture of the situation. Describing it as anything tidier would be inaccurate, and I don't see the use in that.

Both desks are occupied. The hours are what they are.
